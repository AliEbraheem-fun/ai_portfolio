Cross-Attention and Teacher Forcing

Cross-attention implemented from scratch, then an encoder-decoder built on top of it. Teacher Forcing cut the training step from 80.23 ms to 14.13 ms at output length 20, a 5.7x gain on identical arithmetic: greedy decoding runs twenty strictly sequential steps, Teacher Forcing does one call across the full length. The price is exposure bias, since the model only ever sees correct inputs during training. Verified on a reverse-the-sequence task with a known answer: loss fell from 3.007 to 0.0007 over 600 steps and the attention map settled onto the anti-diagonal, against a uniform 0.25 before training.

T5 Span Corruption

T5's pretraining objective rebuilt by hand and checked against what the theory claims. The requested noise ratio holds only on long inputs: 0.15 comes out as 0.154 at 512 tokens but 0.212 at 32, because the loop stops only after the quota is exceeded, so the final span always overshoots. The geometric length distribution puts a third of spans at a single token and barely 2 percent past ten. The lesson's expected output also does not reproduce from a clean random.seed(42): the code is correct, but those exact spans require seven random draws to be consumed first. A seed fixes where a sequence starts, not how much of it a notebook has already used.

NER as Generation (RuMedNER)

Four approaches to one medical NER task, scored on a single metric with honest timing. Fine-tuned BERT wins on every axis at F1 0.426 and 7 ms per example, 131x faster than vLLM and 315x faster than naive generation, and it wins while being the wrong model for the job: bert-base-cased splits Russian into 4.58 subword tokens per word, effectively character level. Qwen3-1.7B under vLLM constrained decoding, given a Pydantic schema and zero training examples, reaches 0.250 and beats a fine-tuned mT5-small at 0.175. Per-class F1 turns out to track vocabulary shape rather than frequency: Drugclass scores 0.718 from 232 training entities while DI scores 0.302 from 995. Three silent failures are documented, none of which raises an exception. StrEnum requires Python 3.11 and the lesson VM ships 3.10, where the obvious backport makes str() return 'A.drugname', so every predicted tag is dropped and F1 becomes exactly 0.000. Constrained decoding guarantees the format but not termination, and 19 of 100 answers ran to the token cap emitting the whitespace a JSON grammar permits. And padding seq2seq targets with pad rather than -100 sends 96 percent of the training signal into predicting emptiness, which is what produces the looping output.

Question Answering with LoRA (SberQuAD)

ruT5-base on the Russian SQuAD analogue, measured three ways against three metrics. Untouched the model scores EM 0.00 and answers every question with '::', since it is a pretrained T5 with no instruction tuning; full fine-tuning takes it to EM 65.08 and F1 82.52, and a rank-8 LoRA on q/k/v reaches 48.21 and 72.28 while training 0.592 percent of the weights. Two findings came out of the gap. LoRA's deficit is largely about span boundaries rather than comprehension: it systematically drops the leading preposition, answering 'Древнем Египте' where the reference is 'в Древнем Египте', which is why the EM gap of 16.9 points is double the F1 gap of 10.2. And LoRA saved only 16.5 percent of VRAM and 17.3 percent of training time, not the usual order of magnitude, because the lesson's optimiser is Adafactor rather than Adam, so the optimiser state LoRA normally eliminates was never being stored, while the backward pass still traverses the frozen network to reach the adapters.

Adapters written from scratch, then run for real on Qwen2.5-0.5B: a low-rank LoRA layer, learnable soft-prompt tokens, and a 4-bit QLoRA base, each checked by measuring peak VRAM rather than trusting the formula. SFT on an Instruction Set: What Four Methods Cost

One dataset, one model, one set of hyperparameters, varying only the training method, with peak VRAM and seconds-per-step measured for each. Full fine-tuning of a 0.6B model at length 1024 runs out of memory on an 8 GB card, so LoRA is not an economy here but the only way to train at all. int8 quantisation then does the opposite of its billing: 5.43 GB against LoRA's 4.61, and 2.2x slower per step, because on a small base the dequantisation overhead outweighs anything saved. Gradient checkpointing was the one knob that behaved as advertised, trading 17 percent of memory for 64 percent more time.

Inference Efficiency: Prompting, Speculation, Quantization

A marketplace case study generating 100 product descriptions under a fixed quality metric, measuring latency and throughput for each optimisation. Decomposing the metric first showed 40 of its 100 points were unreachable by construction, and that the entire real headroom sat in one term. Batching gave 3.6x but the curve turns over at 16 requests as padding eats the gain. bitsandbytes NF4 halved memory and made generation 27 percent slower — the opposite of the AWQ result it is usually quoted alongside, because NF4 dequantises to bf16 on every matmul.
